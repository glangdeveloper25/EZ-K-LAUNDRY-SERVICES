CHAPTER 01

01 — System Overview and Scope

Documentation cutoff: September 30, 2026. September 2026 is the main development period being documented; earlier history is included where evidence exists.

1.1 System identity

The EZ-K Laundry System is a role-based, browser-accessible laundry booking and operations application. Customers prepare laundry bookings, staff process orders and record payments, and the owner manages services, staff, customer records, and reports. The current project runs as an ASP.NET Core MVC application connected directly to a MySQL-compatible relational database.

The application should be described as a booking system with supporting operational and reporting functions. It is not currently a complete accounting package, automated GCash gateway, warehouse inventory system, or multi-branch enterprise platform.

1.2 Business problem and purpose

The supplied discussions identify practical problems encountered while building and demonstrating the application:

  • A booking could appear submitted before the customer finished reviewing or acknowledging the payment step.
  • A customer needed to correct selections before final confirmation rather than create unwanted pending orders.
  • Static or mismatched receipt information could show services the customer had not selected.
  • Showing only one tracked booking was insufficient when the same customer had multiple active orders.
  • Customer-estimated weight could differ from the weight measured at the laundry shop.
  • Completed laundry and received payment were being confused when interpreting monthly income.
  • The owner needed transaction details for a selected date or period, not only a monthly summary.
  • The adviser requested printable records and reports.
  • Redundant actions and excessive header spacing made the dashboards less clear.

These are documented as project concerns, not as results of a formal external survey. No survey sample, interview transcript, statistical finding, or adviser approval is supplied by this package.

1.3 General objective

To organize customer laundry bookings, staff processing activities, service charges, payment recording, and owner reporting within one role-based application, while providing reviewable booking details and printable records.

1.4 Specific objectives and current implementation boundaries

  1. Maintain role-separated access. Provide Customer, Staff, and Owner screens using session-based role checks. Existing checks and remaining security work are described in Chapter 02.
  2. Support deliberate customer booking submission. Collect services, estimated weight, optional add-ons, and payment preference; show a review step with editing/cancellation before persistence.
  3. Record service selections and prices. Use service catalog prices to build booking line items. Saved unit prices preserve the rate applied to that booking rather than automatically repricing old orders when the owner changes the catalog.
  4. Monitor multiple laundry orders. Display active bookings and their current recorded status. Current tracking is loaded with the page; automatic real-time push updates are not implemented.
  5. Allow staff to record actual weight. Recalculate weight-based line quantities while retaining saved unit prices and fixed/per-piece charges. The current design overwrites the item's weight; it does not preserve estimate and actual weight in separate columns.
  6. Distinguish payment recording from laundry processing. Staff-recorded/verified payments support balances and income reporting. A GCash declaration is not bank-verified payment.
  7. Provide owner summaries and transaction reporting. Show paid-payment income grouped by month and support transaction filters. Source-level filter UI defects and pending print validation are explicitly recorded.
  8. Produce printable outputs. Offer print actions for relevant customer and owner records. Individual print scopes differ; printing is not the same as exporting a database backup.

1.6 Main functional modules

Public access and accounts

Public pages include the home page, customer/general login, staff login, customer registration, and initial owner setup. Registration uses validation and password hashing. Customer registration creates a Pending account which staff can approve. Login only admits Active accounts. Role checks are custom session checks, not an ASP.NET Identity user-management implementation.

Customer booking and tracking

The booking form offers Washing, Drying, Folding, and Self Service. Customers enter an estimated weight and can select Powder Soap, Downy Softener, Rush Fee, and Delivery Fee where available in the catalog/form. Review and edit take place before submission. Cash on Pickup and GCash follow different confirmation branches.

The customer dashboard includes active-order tracking, booking history with printing, notifications, rewards-related UI, and account settings. Some displayed metrics/reward content remain placeholders or use unsuitable counts. Therefore, the presence of a card does not establish that the entire module is complete.

Staff operations

The staff area groups pending bookings, current orders, the active queue, customer approvals, walk-in processing, payment recording, and actual-weight editing. Status updates are recorded in history. A standard intended process is Pending, Washing, Drying, Folding, Ready, then Completed; the UI and backend do not consistently enforce every stage or restrict the generic status endpoint to this exact order.

Owner administration

The owner area includes dashboard summaries, staff management, operations listing, customer records and booking details, service pricing, and transaction reporting. The operations list has client-side pagination with 22 rows per page. Add New Staff belongs to the Staff Management section rather than the dashboard overview.

Reports and printing

The Monthly Income Report aggregates recorded Paid payments by month and payment method. Transaction Reports are payment-level records with date/period, method, and status filters. Printable customer history, receipts, operations, customer records, and per-customer booking records support review and submission. Chapter 05 explains exact inclusion rules and print scope.

1.7 End-to-end business sequence

  1. The owner establishes the service catalog and staff accounts.
  2. A customer registers; staff approves the account before login is permitted.
  3. The customer selects services, estimates weight, and chooses optional fees and a payment method.
  4. The system constructs a draft and displays its calculated order summary.
  5. The customer edits or cancels the draft, or proceeds through final confirmation/payment acknowledgement.
  6. The confirmed booking is stored and becomes visible to staff as a pending order.
  7. Staff receives the laundry, measures its actual weight, and records any allowed recalculation.
  8. Staff processes the order and changes its recorded status.
  9. Staff records actual cash received or verifies a declared GCash payment; the booking's paid amount and outstanding balance can then change.
  10. The owner reviews recorded transactions and paid income, optionally filtering and printing the relevant records.

This sequence describes the intended business progression supported by the modules. It does not imply every transition is fully enforced, that all status changes generate notifications, or that a payment gateway is connected.

1.8 Important business distinctions

Estimated amount versus final service amount

A customer estimate is a starting value, not a certified shop measurement. Weight-based service lines use the entered kilograms. When staff records actual weight, the amount may change. A fixed Rush Fee is not multiplied by kilograms; per-piece soap/softener lines use their selected quantity.

Booking state versus payment state

Pending, Washing, Drying, Folding, Ready, Completed, and Cancelled describe laundry processing. Pending, Partial, Paid, Failed, and Refunded are separate payment-status labels in the database. A Completed laundry booking is not automatically proof that money was received. Similarly, a received payment does not by itself prove that laundry is complete.

Booking total versus collected income

The booking total is the sum of service line charges. Collected income in the owner implementation is based on qualifying payment rows. An unpaid cash booking has an order value but no payment transaction until staff records one. The reports are collections summaries, not net-profit calculations: operating expenses and inventory costs are not deducted.

Booking confirmation versus payment declaration

For the current GCash branch, the customer's “I've Made My Payment” action submits a declaration and the booking, not independently verified funds. Staff verification is still required. The current QR is a prototype placeholder, so it must not be presented as a production merchant payment integration.

1.9 Scope exclusions and deferred work

The inspected project does not establish implemented stock-in/stock-out inventory, detergent consumption rules per kilogram, supplier purchasing, verified gateway callbacks, automated bank reconciliation, SMS/email delivery, automatic live push tracking, expense accounting, or multi-shop tenant isolation.

Basic inventory was discussed with the adviser, but implementation is paused at the user's request. Do not include it as a completed September deliverable. Soap and softener being selectable billable services does not mean their physical stock is being monitored.

CHAPTER 02

Architecture, Setup, Security, and Maintenance

Documentation baseline: 30 September 2026. This chapter describes the inspected repository and provides setup instructions for a separate development/test environment. Commands shown below are instructions, not claims that a fresh installation, production deployment, or security assessment was performed during documentation.

1. Application architecture

EZ-K Laundry System is an ASP.NET Core MVC web application. The browser receives server-rendered Razor views. JavaScript handles section switching, modals, form calculations, password visibility, pagination, and browser printing. Controllers receive HTTP requests, validate inputs, query/update MySQL through MySql.Data, prepare view models, and return views or redirects.

It is a single application, not a microservice system or a separate JavaScript single-page application. Most role-dashboard tabs are sections of one Razor view; revealing a tab does not automatically reload its underlying data. Current tracking and notification data is obtained during the dashboard request, without an implemented push or polling service.

The main request sequence is: browser form or navigation request; conventional MVC route; role/session check in a controller; parameterized SQL through a MySQL connection; view-model construction; Razor rendering; browser display. Updates commonly use the POST-redirect-GET pattern. Database transactions are explicitly used around several multi-table operations, including final booking creation, payment recording, actual-weight updates, and status-history writes.

3. Startup, routing, session, and configuration

Program.cs registers MVC views, session, and an HTTP-context accessor. Session is configured for a 30-minute idle timeout, with its cookie marked HttpOnly and essential. Session middleware runs after routing and before authorization middleware. The conventional route defaults to {controller=Home}/{action=Index}/{id?}. Static files are served through the configured static-file/static-asset calls.

The project does not explicitly register an authentication scheme or use UseAuthentication. Role protection is implemented by controller session checks, not by assuming UseAuthorization() alone signs in or protects users. The entry point also does not explicitly show a distributed session-cache registration; verify session-store service availability when reproducing deployment rather than assuming a production-ready distributed store exists.

For non-Development environments, the app uses /Home/Error and HSTS. HTTPS redirection is commented out. The HTTP development profile points to http://localhost:5210; the HTTPS profile includes https://localhost:7128 and the HTTP address. A URL in launchSettings.json is a development convenience, not an internet deployment configuration.

The database connection string currently resides in source in Data/DbConnection.cs. Do not copy its actual credentials into manuscripts, screenshots, repository exports, or troubleshooting messages. A documentation-only placeholder representation is:

Server=<database-host>;Database=<isolated-test-database>;Uid=<application-user>;Pwd=<secret>;

This placeholder is not a claim that environment-variable configuration is already implemented. Moving connection settings into protected configuration and using a restricted application database account are recommended hardening tasks, not changes performed here.

4. Safe local setup on a separate environment

4.1 Preparation

Use a separate project copy and an isolated database server/instance for installation testing. Preserve the working project and current database first. Do not troubleshoot by deleting database tables or importing over real records. Required tools are a compatible .NET 10 SDK, a compatible MySQL/MariaDB server, and an SQL administration client; use the actual server/version approved for the project and test its constraint behavior.

The SQL file contains both CREATE DATABASE IF NOT EXISTS laundry_final and USE laundry_final. Selecting a different database in a graphical SQL client does not override that embedded USE command. Either run the original script only on an isolated server intended for this installation, or prepare/review an explicitly renamed copy before import. Do not change/import the live schema merely to follow these notes.

4.2 Schema initialization

Inspect the script before executing it in the isolated environment. It creates InnoDB tables, uses utf8mb4, inserts reference data, and defines three aggregate views. It does not create a default login user because application-generated password hashes are expected.

Although the script does not drop tables and uses CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS, it is not read-only or a harmless schema checker. Its ON DUPLICATE KEY UPDATE seed statements can replace existing reference descriptions, service prices, and other lookup properties. CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW also rewrites view definitions. Re-importing can reset configured prices. IF NOT EXISTS will not migrate the columns of an already-existing incompatible table.

The script's SET time_zone = '+08:00' affects that SQL session. Do not assume it permanently configures every connection the application later opens. See the reporting chapter for date-boundary implications.

4.3 Application startup

In the separate project copy, point the connection helper at the isolated database using safe local credentials. Keep secrets out of documentation and screenshots. Then execute the following commands individually from that copy's project directory:

dotnet --info
dotnet restore
dotnet build
dotnet run --launch-profile http

restore obtains declared packages; build compiles C# and Razor; run starts the development application. These steps write build artifacts and can use network access. They were not run as part of this chapter's creation. If port 5210 is occupied, identify the existing application before stopping anything; do not terminate unrelated processes.

Open the displayed local application address and verify the home and login pages. A successful login-page response proves reachability only, not correct booking or financial processing. Test database connectivity and the isolated account setup before broader workflow tests.

4.4 Initial accounts and usable services

On a fresh database, use the Owner setup route while no Owner account exists. Sign in as Owner, create Staff through Staff Management, and verify the active service prices. Self Service is inserted/activated by the owner's pricing update and requires a positive price to be available to customers.

Register a dedicated test Customer through the application. The new account is Pending until Staff approves it. After approval, use separate browser profiles/sessions for Owner, Staff, and Customer when testing cross-role behavior. Do not seed plaintext passwords or reuse personal credentials. Record test references separately from real shop transactions.

5. Security controls present in source

5.1 Password handling

PasswordSecurity requires 8–128 characters with uppercase, lowercase, numeric, and non-alphanumeric characters. Hashing uses PBKDF2-SHA256 with 100,000 iterations, a random 16-byte salt, and a 32-byte derived hash. The stored format includes algorithm prefix, iteration count, Base64 salt, and Base64 hash. Verification derives the candidate and compares using CryptographicOperations.FixedTimeEquals.

This is a description of the implementation, not a certification that its settings satisfy every current security standard. Registration, Staff creation, and password-change flows call password-policy/hash helpers. The password eye control only changes browser visibility. Stored hashes and salts should still be treated as sensitive account data and excluded from published documentation.

5.2 Request and database protections

Account, Customer, Staff, and Owner controllers carry AutoValidateAntiforgeryToken. MVC form Tag Helpers are available through _ViewImports, and mutating workflows generally use POST forms. Queries use parameters for user-supplied values. Some query structure is assembled from server-controlled whitelists; this is distinct from concatenating raw user input into SQL.

Customer booking/history/notification queries restrict records by the logged-in customer ID. Staff/Owner mutations check the relevant role. Payment recording and actual-weight changes lock selected bookings while calculating amounts and writing related records. Database foreign keys and check constraints add another integrity layer. A transaction protects participating statements, not every business rule everywhere in the application.

5.3 Limits and hardening needs

No login rate limiting, MFA, email verification, forgotten-password delivery, or full login/audit monitoring was found. Session role checks do not re-query account status on each request, so deactivating Staff does not demonstrate immediate revocation of an existing session. Logout is a GET-reachable session-clearing action. Owner initial registration is guarded by an existence query, not a demonstrated concurrency-safe setup lock.

Some endpoints enforce only a subset of expected validations. General status updating can bypass stricter dedicated completion/cancellation rules; profile editing has weaker validation than registration; duplicate partial payment submissions are not fully idempotent. Details appear in the workflow and limitations chapters. Do not describe the project as penetration-tested, production-hardened, or error-free without separate evidence.

Before public deployment, review HTTPS enforcement, cookie security settings, trusted host/reverse-proxy setup, secret storage, database privileges, account/session revocation, centralized authorization, input validation, and safe error logging. These recommendations do not authorize changing the current application during this documentation task.

6. External resources and offline behavior

Bootstrap CSS/JavaScript, jQuery, and validation libraries are referenced locally under wwwroot/lib. The shared layout loads Bootstrap Icons from a CDN, Google Fonts, and SweetAlert2 from a CDN. The payment screen loads an external GCash logo and QR image. An offline classroom demo can therefore lose icons, fonts, alerts, or payment-screen imagery even if the local app/database is running.

The current QR encodes sample text, not a verified merchant payment request. No payment gateway SDK/callback or SMS-provider integration was found. Public layout/FAQ copy mentions SMS, real-time tracking, automatic rush prioritization, and no delivery, while implemented screens include a Delivery Fee and database-backed refresh-on-load tracking. These text claims require reconciliation with actual scope. Queue-loading SQL currently orders by booking time, not an implemented rush-priority scheduling algorithm.

7. Maintenance, backups, and handoff

Back up source, the schema, and a controlled database export before migrations or major changes. A source-code archive is not a database backup. Store backups with limited access because customer names, contact details, payment records, notifications, and password hashes are sensitive. Test restoring to an isolated server; a backup is not verified solely because a file exists. No automated backup/restore module is implemented in this repository.

Maintain a change log with actual dates, affected files, database changes, test results, and known limitations. Do not use copied file timestamps as guaranteed development dates. Keep screenshots and test data anonymized when used in a school manuscript. Package only the intended source/documentation, not credentials, personal data exports, or arbitrary tmp/build outputs.

The application currently has no automated test project or migration history framework. Use the supplied manual test plan, record outcomes, and add automated coverage incrementally. A clean build, successful page load, and source review are useful checks but do not establish complete payment, report, role-security, browser-print, or multi-user correctness.

1. Purpose, scope, and evidence

The database supports account approval, customer and walk-in bookings, selected laundry services and add-ons, laundry processing, payment recording, notifications, rewards transactions, and owner reporting. This chapter describes what is actually defined in the repository, distinguishing database capability from functionality exposed by the application.

Primary sources:

  • CustomerController.cs: customer draft finalization, receipts, notifications, and reward redemption.
  • StaffController.cs: walk-in creation, status updates, actual-weight recording, and payments.
  • OwnerController.cs: account administration, pricing, and report queries.
  • PasswordSecurity.cs: application-generated password hashes.

The SQL file contains 15 tables and 3 views. Its header describes a MySQL 8.0 / MariaDB-compatible design. The installed database engine/version and enforcement of constraints have not been independently verified for this documentation. No database rows were changed or exported during preparation of this chapter. Historical creation and deployment dates cannot be established from the schema text alone; September 30 is the documentation date, not an invented database implementation date.

2. Physical design and normalization

The script targets database laundry_final, uses utf8mb4 with utf8mb4_unicode_ci, defines InnoDB tables, and sets the importing connection's time zone to +08:00. That SET time_zone statement does not, by itself, configure every later application connection.

Reference tables store role, account-status, booking-status, payment-method, payment-status, and item-type labels once. Transaction tables reference their identifiers instead of copying these labels into every row. Bookings are separated from physical laundry items, purchased service lines, payments, and history. This avoids repeated customer details and supports several service lines and payment records for one booking.

The design is intended to support third normal form: non-key attributes describe the entity represented by their table; reusable descriptions are separated; many-to-many booking/service selections use a linking table. booking_services.unit_price is an intentional historical sale-price snapshot, not a duplicate of the current catalogue price with the same meaning. A later owner price change should affect new selections, not overwrite the unit price of existing booked lines.

The design does not include inventory balances, stock movements, suppliers, product purchase costs, dosing rules, or a stock-usage ledger. A consumable service line is a charge to a customer, not proof of remaining stock.

4. Referential integrity and deletion effects

All declared FKs use ON UPDATE CASCADE. Delete behavior deliberately varies:

  • RESTRICT protects reference records in use, customers owning bookings, and bookings with payment records.
  • CASCADE removes booking item/service/history rows and booking-linked notifications if a booking is actually deleted. User-linked notifications and reward movements also cascade with their user.
  • SET NULL preserves a booking/payment/history record when its optional actor is deleted, and preserves reward movements when their optional booking is deleted.

Cancellation in the application is a status change, not physical deletion of a booking. It preserves customer history. However, FK cascades do not constitute a permanent immutable audit archive. Backup, retention, and any administrative deletion policy need separate decisions.

6. Views and calculation rules

v_booking_totals

One row per booking: booking_id, booking_code, and order_total. Its formula is SUM(booking_services.quantity * unit_price), defaulting to zero when no line exists. The booking table does not store a separate total that can drift from its lines.

v_booking_payment_balances

One row per booking: booking identifiers, order_total, amount_paid, and balance_due. The view counts payment rows whose status is Partial or Paid, then subtracts that sum from the order total. It does not clamp a negative balance to zero.

Current inconsistency: Staff balance calculations, the actual-weight guard, and Owner earnings count Paid only. The schema view therefore disagrees with those controllers if a Partial-status row is introduced. Current staff installments are individually recorded as Paid even when the booking still has a balance. Decide and standardize the meaning of Partial before relying on externally entered Partial records.

v_user_reward_balances

One row per user: user_id and reward_points = SUM(points), defaulting to zero. This supports a ledger-derived balance without a duplicated balance column. The view's presence does not prove that all reward-earning rules or displayed balances are connected to it.

The inspected controllers compute their own aggregates; they do not call these three views. A schema view and a page with a similar label are not automatically the same data path.

7. Database-to-application write workflows

Customer booking

The customer selects services and reviews a server-created draft kept in session. The draft is not yet a bookings row. Finalization writes, within one database transaction: Pending booking, one Clothes item, selected booking-service lines, optional Pending GCash declaration, and initial status-history event. Customer Cash-on-Pickup booking creates no payment record until staff records received money. Duplicate-key handling looks up an existing booking using the same draft code/customer after rollback; it is not a general guarantee against every duplicate submission.

The GCash declaration amount is currently the draft's whole service total. Despite method names such as ProcessPartialPayment, this is not an integrated gateway verification or a documented percentage-deposit calculation. Staff verification is separate.

Staff actual-weight adjustment

Within a transaction the code locks the booking and first Clothes item, rejects terminal bookings, calculates the revised service total using saved sale prices, and rejects a total below already Paid amounts. It changes weight_kg and the quantity of all PerKg service lines, leaving PerItem/Flat quantities unchanged. It appends a same-status history event and a booking-linked customer notification, then commits.

The original estimate is overwritten in the structured item field; the audit note retains the previous/new values as text. The schema does not record a separate immutable estimate, explicit actual-weight timestamp, or structured weighed-by field. Existing Pending GCash declaration amounts are not automatically recalculated by this action.

Staff payment recording

The code locks the booking, accepts Cash/GCash only, rejects cancelled bookings and invalid or excessive amounts, and totals existing Paid rows. It either verifies the oldest Pending GCash row or inserts a new Paid row, with recorder and paid_at. Paying the remaining balance in Cash marks existing Pending GCash declarations Failed with an explanatory note. A customer payment notification is inserted before commit.

That payment notification currently contains the booking code in its text but does not populate its optional booking_id. A bank/GCash reference is not required or captured by this payment action. Do not describe the feature as automatic bank reconciliation.

Laundry status changes

Controller operations update the current status and append history in a transaction. Some terminal-action paths check allowed source states, while the general update path does not impose the full ordered lifecycle. There are no database triggers enforcing state progression, generating notifications for every transition, or requiring full payment before Completed. “Completed laundry” and “Paid payment” remain separate concepts.

2.1 Customer registration

Names accept letters, spaces, apostrophes, and hyphens. Email or phone already used by another user is rejected. 3. Customer booking: draft before submission

The central distinction is between a draft order in the session and a persisted booking in MySQL. Opening Book now, reviewing an order, or viewing the payment QR screen must not be documented as creating a booking; the code has later persistence points.

3.1 Available choices and server validation

The customer booking form presents Washing, Drying, Folding, and Self Service. Multiple services may be selected. Estimated weight is initially 5 kg, with server acceptance from 1 through 20 kg. Optional extras are Powder Soap, Downy Softener, Rush Fee, and Delivery. Powder and Downy quantity must be from 1 through 20 when selected. Payment selection is GCash or Cash on Pickup, submitted as GCash or Cash.

Self Service is retained. It is treated as a per-kilogram service in the owner price setup and is disabled for customer selection when its loaded price is not positive. This is a bookable service option, not a machine reservation or machine-control subsystem.

Prices come from active rows in services. CreateBookingDraft looks up each selected name and obtains its ID, charging basis, and current price from the database. It does not trust a browser-supplied total. PerKg quantity becomes the estimated weight, selected PerItem extras use their entered quantities, and flat services use quantity 1. An unavailable service prevents creation of the draft.

Source: ConfirmBooking, CreateBookingDraft in CustomerController.

3.2 Review, edit, and cancel

Despite its name, ConfirmBooking is the first POST from Review Order and stores a JSON draft in the session under CustomerBookingDraft. It then redirects to ReviewBooking. The review page lists only the draft's selected service lines, weight, payment method, and estimated total, together with a warning that measured weight can affect the final amount.

Edit Order returns to the booking form with draft selections restored. The customer can revise services, weight, extras, quantities, and method and review again. Cancel Booking clears the draft without inserting a booking. The QR screen also provides a return-to-review link and a draft-cancel action. Merely using browser Back does not itself call cancellation; it navigates browser history. Nevertheless, no saved booking exists until the corresponding final submission below.

The draft is session-bound. A missing/expired draft on the review or QR GET route silently redirects to the booking form; the final-submission POST routes also set an expiry/error message. It is not a permanent saved cart. Multiple open browser tabs share the same session draft, so simultaneous booking edits require specific testing.

4. Staff booking and laundry operations

4.1 Queues and lifecycle

The Staff dashboard separately loads Pending bookings and an active queue of Washing, Drying, Folding, and Ready bookings. Accepting a customer booking sets it to Washing and adds status history. Processing-status updates write the new current status and a corresponding history row in a transaction. Completed and Cancelled records remain in historical records but disappear from these active queues.

The normal intended sequence is Pending, Washing, Drying, Folding, Ready, and Completed, with cancellation as an alternative terminal result. Current enforcement is less strict than that sequence: the general UpdateStatus action accepts a whitelist including Completed and Cancelled and calls a helper that does not validate the prior state. Separate CompleteOrder and CancelOrder actions do impose stricter rules: completion from Ready only, and cancellation from a listed nonterminal state with a 3–150 character reason. Those stricter rules are not enforced uniformly by every status-changing route.

The active-card dropdown presently offers Washing, Drying, and Ready, not Folding, even though Folding exists in the schema, controller whitelist, and customer progress display. Acceptance always starts at Washing even for a booking that did not select Washing. These are workflow limitations, not service charges added automatically by the tracking graphic.

Source: AcceptBooking, UpdateStatus, ChangeBookingStatus, CompleteOrder, CancelOrder, ChangeTerminalStatus in StaffController; Staff view.

4.2 Recording actual measured weight

Staff has Record / Edit Actual Weight controls on relevant booking views. The modal shows current weight, current total, paid amount, and a projected total. On submission UpdateActualWeight:

  1. Requires a Staff session and positive weight no greater than 999.99 kg.
  2. Locks the booking and rejects missing or terminal bookings.
  3. Selects and locks the first Clothes item belonging to that booking.
  4. Calculates the new amount using actual weight for each PerKg line and existing quantities for other lines.
  5. Rejects a projected total lower than the already-Paid amount, within the small comparison tolerance used by the code.
  6. Updates the item weight and the quantities of PerKg service lines while preserving their saved unit prices.
  7. Adds an audit-style status-history note with old/new weight and recalculated total, creates a customer notification, and commits the transaction.

Example only: Washing at PHP 25/kg plus Drying at PHP 25/kg and one PHP 10 softener has a PHP 260 estimate at 5 kg. At measured 6 kg the new total is PHP 310. The softener remains PHP 10. These are illustrative rates, not a statement of the current shop price list.

The schema currently uses the same item weight column for the estimate and later actual weight. There is no separate immutable estimated-weight field, measured-weight field, or explicit weight-verification flag. Previous values survive in the text history note, not as separately queryable weight columns. Multiple laundry-item weighing is not implemented by this action. A pending GCash declaration is not automatically rewritten by a weight change; staff must verify the amount genuinely received.

4.3 Payment recording and balance

RecordPaymentCore accepts numeric booking ID, BK-<id>, #BK-<id>, or full EZK reference. It verifies positive amount and Cash/GCash method, locks the matching booking, rejects Cancelled bookings, totals saved service lines, and subtracts only payments with status Paid. It refuses already-settled bookings and amounts above the remaining balance.

For GCash, the earliest Pending GCash row is updated to Paid if one exists; otherwise a new Paid row is inserted. The verified amount, recording Staff user, and paid timestamp are saved. Cash inserts a Paid row. If cash settles the remaining balance, outstanding Pending GCash declarations are marked Failed with a superseded-by-cash note. A payment notification is inserted for the customer in the same transaction.

Actual installments are supported as multiple received payment records. Each received installment is stored as Paid, even when the order still has a balance. The booking's payment balance must therefore be distinguished from an individual payment row's status. The controller does not use the Partial status for these installments.

Payment entry is a staff attestation, not payment-provider confirmation. A balance guard does not prevent every duplicate partial entry: a repeated smaller payment can still fit within the remaining balance. Independent transaction-reference validation and complete retry/idempotency coverage remain unverified. Completing laundry does not automatically record payment, and the completion action does not enforce zero remaining balance.

4.4 Walk-in processing and fulfillment

ProcessWalkIn finds a user by phone or creates an Active walk-in Customer, creates a Washing booking attributed to Staff, records Clothes weight and selected service lines, and adds initial history. It recalculates prices server-side instead of trusting the submitted total. It does not create a received payment automatically.

CHAPTER 05

Reporting and Printing

Documentation baseline: 30 September 2026. Reporting behavior below is based on the current controller/view code. This chapter records an important filter-interface defect and identifies print checks still required; it does not claim that every filter or printed page is acceptance-tested.

1. Why there are different kinds of reports

A booking records the customer's requested laundry work. Its order amount is the sum of booked service quantities multiplied by their saved unit prices. A payment records a financial event attached to that booking. One booking can have no payment, one payment, or several payments. Processing status and payment status are separate concepts.

Consequently, an order labelled Completed can still have no recorded receipt of money. An amount in Booking History or Laundry Operations is not proof that the amount was paid. Conversely, a received deposit can count in income while the laundry is still being processed. The current system reports recorded receipts, not accounting profit: supplier expenses, inventory consumption costs, operating expenses, and a comprehensive refund ledger are not implemented in these reports.

3. Monthly Income Report

OwnerController.LoadIncomeReport calculates Total Earnings from every payment with payment status Paid. Its separate monthly query includes only Paid rows whose paid_at is not null, groups them by payment year/month, and sorts the newest month first. Each month shows Cash, GCash, Other, and Total Income. Other means a method whose name is neither Cash nor GCash.

The monthly view does not require that a booking be Completed. It does not sum bookings or quote estimates. Months without qualifying payment rows are omitted instead of appearing automatically as zero-filled calendar months. When no qualifying records exist, the view displays No paid transactions recorded yet.

The monthly summary and the detailed Transaction Report are separate views. Applying a transaction filter does not filter the Overview monthly table or the all-time Total Earnings card.

3.1 Why an income report may be empty

Common source-consistent explanations are:

  1. Only bookings were created; no payment has been recorded by Staff.
  2. Customer GCash declarations are still Pending and have not been verified.
  3. Payments have another status, such as Failed, rather than Paid.
  4. A Paid row has no paid_at, so the monthly query omits it even though the all-time earnings query can include it.

Do not fill the report by automatically declaring completed bookings paid or inserting fabricated historical receipts. Staff should enter/verify actual receipts, and historical corrections should be based on real evidence.

3.2 Partial payment versus the Partial status

If a PHP 300 booking receives PHP 100, the Staff entry creates a Paid payment for PHP 100, leaving an order balance of PHP 200. The received PHP 100 therefore contributes to income. A separate payment row literally marked Partial does not contribute, because the report predicates check for Paid. Current Staff recording does not create Partial-status rows; that lookup status is available in the schema/report filter, but has different semantics from a partly settled booking.

4. Transaction Report fields and query

5. Filters and date semantics

The Owner filter form submits a GET request to Index with openReports=true. Its supported values are as follows.

Different categories combine with AND. For example, Today plus GCash plus Paid means only today's effective-dated GCash rows marked Paid. Selecting All removes the date restriction, not any method/status restriction the user still has selected.

The controller contains filtering logic, but this UI defect means arbitrary dates/months cannot yet be represented as fully usable through the visible controls. Default hidden input values can still be submitted. Today, Yesterday, and All do not need those additional fields, but still require runtime checks. This issue was recorded during documentation, not repaired as part of the documentation request.

6. Totals shown above the transaction table

The Transactions card counts all returned payment rows, regardless of status. Paid Cash, Paid GCash, and Paid Other cards sum only returned rows whose status equals Paid. Paid Total is their sum.

For example, a result set with one PHP 200 Paid Cash row and one PHP 100 Pending GCash row has two Transactions and PHP 200 Paid Total. Filtering to Pending can correctly show records while every Paid summary is zero. Amounts should not be blindly summed across all statuses as income.

Refunded rows are not treated as negative cash outflow in the paid-only totals. If a historical Paid row is changed to Refunded, these queries exclude it instead of displaying a refund ledger entry. There is no implemented refund-processing workflow in the examined controllers; do not label the report net revenue or profit.

Use controlled test accounts and clearly identified test transactions, not fabricated production payments. Record expected versus actual results and test dates separately from this baseline documentation.

  3. Staff verification changes that declaration to Paid and contributes exactly the verified amount.
  4. Two real installments on one booking produce two payment rows without multiplying either by the number of services.

  6. Custom start/end days include transactions near midnight correctly; Today/Yesterday labels agree with database date boundaries.

  8. Screen totals match print totals for Paid-only and mixed-status results.
  9. Operations printing with more than 22 test bookings includes all rows while the on-screen page stays paginated afterward.
  10. Long customer names, service lists, reference codes, empty datasets, and multipage reports remain readable in print preview/PDF.

CHAPTER 08

Known Limitations, Source Findings, and Future Work

Review date: 30 September 2026
Scope: Current repository implementation; not a live-database audit or executed penetration test.

This chapter prevents the manuscript from claiming functionality beyond the evidence. It records source-observed gaps and separates them from optional extensions. No application code was changed while preparing this documentation.

1. Priority meaning

  • P1 — resolve or explicitly disclose before acceptance: Affects correctness of amounts, requested behavior, state transitions, or report usability.
  • P2 — address before operational deployment or broader demonstration: Affects reliability, trust in displayed information, scalability, or security hardening.
  • P3 — optional/approval-dependent extension: Expands the product scope; not automatically authorized by this documentation task.

Priority is an assessment for planning, not a verified production incident or a claim that data was lost. Test IDs refer to the Acceptance Test Plan.

2. P1 findings: correctness and promised workflow

L01 — Conditional report filter fields remain hidden

Evidence: Views/Owner/Index.cshtml defines .report-period-field { display: none; }. updateReportPeriodFields() assigns an empty inline display value to fields for the selected period.

Why it matters: Removing the inline display does not override the class rule. The date/month/year/custom-range input containers therefore remain hidden even when the matching period is chosen. The controller accepts the filter parameters, but that does not establish usable filtering through the form.

Required follow-up: Correct the visibility rule; test Specific Date, Monthly, Yearly, and Custom in the browser; confirm submitted values and printed scope. Do not claim these controls passed UI testing until T35–T36 are executed.

L02 — Walk-in soap/softener names disagree with service lookup

Evidence: Walk-in JavaScript in Views/Staff/Index.cshtml sends Soap Powder and Downy Softener. StaffController.ProcessWalkIn normalizes only Rush to Rush Fee; AddBookingService looks up an exact active service_name and returns zero if not found. Seeded/owner-maintained names are Powder and Downy.

Impact: The screen can add an add-on cost to its estimate while persistence skips that line and reports a lower server total. Unknown tokens are also silently skipped instead of rejecting the incomplete selection.

Required follow-up: Use consistent identifiers or a service-ID catalog, reject unknown services, and retest persisted quantities/totals (T43). Keep server-side price calculation; do not trust a hidden client total as the remedy.

L03 — Status update paths do not enforce a single state-transition policy

Evidence: Dedicated CompleteOrder accepts only Ready; dedicated CancelOrder accepts selected active states and requires a reason. UpdateStatus accepts a list including Completed and Cancelled, then calls ChangeBookingStatus, which updates a booking by ID without checking its previous status. AcceptBooking also calls the generic helper.

Impact: A crafted or stale request can bypass the stricter completion/cancellation path, move stages backwards, or alter an already terminal booking. Generic transitions do not verify whether the selected laundry service includes that stage. The UI alone is not a complete state machine.

There is also an interface mismatch: the staff phase dropdown offers Washing, Drying, and Ready but omits Folding, although UpdateStatus accepts Folding and the customer tracking view displays it. A controller capability must not be described as an available staff dropdown choice.

Required follow-up: Define allowed transitions once and enforce them server-side for every endpoint; decide whether completion requires payment or allows credit; require cancellation reason on every path. Test T19–T21. Until then, document status changes as staff-managed updates, not a fully validated workflow engine.

L04 — Several dashboard metrics do not match their labels

L05 — GCash page is a prototype declaration, not a payment gateway

Evidence: Views/Customer/PaymentQR.cshtml obtains a QR image whose data payload is the literal text EZKLaundryPartialPay. ProcessPartialPayment creates a Pending declaration; it does not contact GCash or verify a merchant transaction.

Impact: The page cannot be represented as automatically processing or confirming actual GCash payment. Its title says “Scan to Pay Partial” while the pending record is populated with the whole estimated order total; no explicit deposit amount/percentage or uploaded proof is captured there.

Required follow-up: For a prototype, clearly label manual payment instructions and staff verification. Before real use, establish a legitimate merchant payment method and verification/reference workflow, then test settlement and failure cases. Never transfer real funds to a placeholder. L06 — Partial-payment semantics differ between database view and controllers

Evidence: SQL v_booking_payment_balances counts statuses Partial and Paid as collected amounts. Staff balance, actual-weight checks, owner earnings, and report summary code count only Paid. Staff recording stores any accepted installment amount as a Paid payment row.

Impact: A PHP 100 installment can be a Paid transaction while the booking still has an unpaid balance; that is different from a row whose payment status is named Partial. Imported or manually prepared Partial rows can cause the SQL view and the application to disagree.

Required follow-up: Define payment-row status versus overall booking settlement. Harmonize the SQL view, controllers, fixtures, and manuscript. Do not merge the words “partially paid order” and “Partial payment status” without explanation. Test T22 and T34.

L07 — Paid rows without paid_at can produce unequal report totals

Evidence: LoadIncomeReport Total Earnings sums all Paid rows. Its monthly query also requires paid_at IS NOT NULL. Transaction reporting uses COALESCE(p.paid_at, p.created_at).

Impact: A legacy/imported Paid row lacking a payment date appears in earnings and transaction totals but not monthly income. The normal staff path sets paid_at; the risk concerns inconsistent existing/imported data and database constraints that do not require a date for Paid status.

Required follow-up: Audit such rows without inventing dates; require meaningful payment dates or disclose unallocated collections. Reconcile all three report totals (T34).

3. P2 findings: reliability, information design, and deployment readiness

L08 — Tracking and notifications are page-load snapshots

CustomerController.Index reads the database when requested. The customer view switches already-loaded sections; it contains no polling, server-sent events, or WebSocket update mechanism. Staff changes appear after a reload/navigation that fetches data, not necessarily while the customer's open page sits idle.

The Notifications view renders message cards as non-clickable containers. There is no implemented notification detail/open action or per-message mark-read navigation. “Mark all as read” is implemented, but that is different from opening a notification. Newest 100 messages are loaded, and the unread badge counts only this loaded subset.

Generic status changes do not currently insert notifications. Actual-weight updates and payment recording do. A staff broadcast endpoint inserts a generic notice for active customers; it is not an SMS, email, mobile push, or custom message service.

Next decision: Keep refresh-based behavior and label it accurately, or approve automatic refresh/click-through work. Tests T18–T19 and T29–T31.

L09 — Actual weight overwrites one stored weight rather than preserving two fields

UpdateActualWeight updates the first Clothes item and all PerKg service quantities. The prior weight is included in status-history notes, but there are no separate structured estimated_weight, actual_weight, weighed_at, and weighed_by columns in the current implementation.

Consequently, the current item weight becomes the actual weight and the original estimate is no longer directly available as a separate field for comparison/reporting. The helper assumes one Clothes item; it is not a general multi-item weighing workflow. Weight changes below collected money are blocked rather than initiating refunds/credit handling.

Next decision: If the manuscript requires distinct estimate/actual tracking or dispute resolution, preserve both structured values and actor/time metadata. Do not claim those fields already exist. Tests T26–T28.

L10 — A payment lock is not complete duplicate-request protection

RecordPaymentCore locks the booking and checks its remaining Paid balance. This is useful protection against overpayment and concurrent full settlement. It has no unique request/idempotency key, however. Repeating a smaller payment while enough balance remains may create another Paid row. The optional database transaction reference is not captured in this staff form.

Customer booking confirmation has different protection: the draft carries a code; bookings.booking_code is unique; duplicate-key handling retrieves the existing customer booking. This should be tested under concurrent requests rather than described as an all-system guarantee.

Next decision: Define which repeated requests are duplicates versus separate installments; capture verified payment references where applicable. Tests T16 and T25.

L11 — Cancellation, completion, payment, and refund are separate concepts

Completing a laundry order does not create a Paid payment. Cancelling one does not reverse an existing Paid payment. Payment-status rows include Refunded/Failed/Partial, but the application does not provide a complete refund approval/execution ledger or reversal workflow.

Therefore, the Monthly Income Report represents amounts currently recorded Paid, not necessarily net cash after refunds and not business profit. Laundry Operations is a booking list, while Transaction Report is a payment-row list. An unpaid Cash booking without a payment row will not appear in Transaction Report.

Next decision: Approve refund and release-with-balance policies before expanding behavior. Do not create fake historical payments to make a report nonempty. Tests T21–T24 and T32–T38.

L12 — Customer rush and delivery fees do not set fulfillment metadata

Customer draft persistence inserts booking code, customer, and Pending status, then items/services. Selecting Rush Fee or Delivery Fee creates a billable line but does not also populate bookings.is_rush, fulfillment_method, delivery_address, or a pickup schedule. Database defaults therefore remain relevant, including DropOff and false rush.

Staff pickup-method screens can display fulfillment data already present in the database, but this is not proof that customer scheduling/address entry is implemented end-to-end. A fee selection is not equivalent to an arranged delivery.

Next decision: Link selections to explicit fulfillment requirements only after confirming the desired booking scope. Review rush counts and logistics reports accordingly.

L13 — Price updates and draft pricing need an explicit policy

Owner updates write individual service prices without wrapping the full set in one transaction. A mid-operation failure can leave a partially updated catalog. Customer drafts store selected IDs, basis, quantities, and prices at review time; final confirmation does not reread whether catalog prices or availability changed in the meantime.

Saved booking unit prices correctly preserve an order-specific price snapshot. The remaining decision is whether a review-time quote is guaranteed until confirmation or must expire/revalidate. Current session idle timeout is not a formal published quote-validity policy.

Next decision: Define quote validity, atomic catalog update behavior, and any service-availability recheck; test T11. Do not silently alter historical booking prices to synchronize them with the current catalog.

L14 — Service selection and process stages are not fully reconciled

Customer service selection is multi-select, including Self Service. The server confirms names exist and are active but does not enforce a strict core-service list or an incompatibility rule between self-service and staff-operated services. Tracking always displays Washing, Drying, Folding, and Ready, even for a booking that did not purchase all those stages.

This does not prove extra services are charged: totals come from saved service lines. It does mean the process presentation can confuse a customer who selected only Washing or Drying.

Next decision: Define whether stages describe physical workflow or purchased services; present them consistently. Validate add-on-only/forged service selections and service combinations (T09–T10, T18).

L15 — Print controls exist; complete print acceptance remains pending

Owner print CSS has dedicated sections for monthly income, operations, customer records, per-customer booking modal, and transactions. Customer history/receipt also have print controls. Operations printing intentionally reveals all loaded rows rather than the current 22-row screen page.

The receipt's Print Receipt control calls window.print(), but Views/Customer/BookingReceipt.cshtml has no receipt-specific print CSS to hide its dashboard/print action row. Therefore, the button establishes access to browser printing, not a verified clean receipt-only layout. Hiding actions is an acceptance target, not a current proven behavior.

Transaction output uses a wide table and a responsive overflow container. Unlike the monthly report's explicit print overflow rule, the transaction container has no equivalent override in its dedicated print rules. Long names, long service lists, nowrap columns, browser-specific paging, and modal heights still need visual PDF/print checks.

Filter changes are only applied after form submission; printing immediately after changing an unsent dropdown prints the last rendered result. A Print dialog opening is insufficient proof that all rows/columns are included.

Next decision: Execute T39–T42 on supported browsers and paper sizes; save sanitized evidence. Do not mark printing fully accepted based on source inspection alone.

L16 — Scalability is limited by full-data dashboard loading

Owner Index loads staff, customers, all bookings, per-customer bookings through individual queries, monthly income, and filtered payment transactions for one dashboard request. Operations pagination is client-side; it does not reduce database row loading. Transaction Report has no server-side pagination/streamed export. Customer history and active lists are also loaded for rendering.

Impact: Larger datasets can increase query count, memory, response time, and print size. This is a capacity risk, not a measured performance result.

Next decision: Establish realistic volume targets and response-time measurements before redesigning queries/pagination. Keep all-row printing/export semantics clear if screen paging becomes server-side.

L17 — Authentication is prototype session-based and needs hardening review

Positive controls present: role checks, customer-scoped queries, controller anti-forgery validation, parameterized SQL, PBKDF2-SHA256 password storage with random salts, and fixed-time hash comparison. These should be described accurately rather than dismissed.

Remaining source-observed considerations:

  • Role helpers primarily trust existing session values; deactivating a user does not recheck account status on every protected request.
  • No application login rate limiter, account lockout, MFA, password reset/email verification workflow, or centralized authorization policy is established in the inspected code.
  • Logout is a GET action that clears session state.
  • HTTPS redirection is commented out in Program.cs; local HTTP demonstrations should not be represented as production-secure deployment.
  • Database connection configuration is embedded in application source. Actual credentials must not be reproduced in documentation; move deployment secrets into protected configuration and use a least-privilege database account.
  • Staff addition and profile updates do not apply exactly the same model validation path as customer registration; unique data failures and malformed contact/email handling need uniform tests.
  • Owner initialization checks whether an owner exists before insertion; concurrency and secure first-run access need consideration before public deployment.

Next decision: Complete deployment/security review and T01–T04/T07 before public exposure. This documentation does not assert that an exploit was attempted or that production security has been certified.

L18 — Staff deletion can remove useful actor attribution

OwnerController.DeleteStaff deletes the staff user row. Several historical actor relationships use ON DELETE SET NULL, retaining bookings/payments/history while losing the staff foreign-key reference. Transaction Report then may show the fallback label Customer/System when recorder identity is absent; that fallback does not establish who actually performed the action.

Next decision: Prefer a clearly defined retention/archive policy if accountability is required. Reusing Inactive status may be more appropriate than deletion, but this is a proposed change, not a performed one. Test with disposable data only (T07).

L19 — Loyalty screens and ledger do not form a complete verified reward lifecycle

The database has reward_transactions and a derived balance view. RedeemReward sums ledger points and inserts a negative redemption when sufficient. Displayed customer points remain fixed at 1,250, and a complete automatic earn-on-completion/payment path was not established in the inspected controllers.

Redemption accepts a submitted reward name and assigns 800 points for exactly Free Wash, otherwise 200; it does not use a dedicated reward catalog/strict name allowlist. Concurrent redemption balance checks lack a clearly locked user/balance record.

Next decision: Either limit demonstration claims to the existing ledger prototype or approve a full earn/redeem/catalog/concurrency integration. Never promise a displayed 1,250-point balance is spendable without checking the actual ledger (T44).

L20 — Date/time conventions are mixed

Booking codes use DateTime.UtcNow. UI relative times and default report labels use local DateTime.Now/Today. SQL dates use database NOW() and CURDATE(). Transaction grouping uses payment timestamps; it does not use the date portion embedded in booking codes.

Impact: Near midnight, a code's date, displayed relative time, and Today filter can disagree if application/database time zones differ. The code date is not evidence of the booking's local business day by itself.

Next decision: Establish a documented business timezone and storage/display conversion rule; test midnight boundaries and end-of-month reports (T33–T36). Historical project-work dates must be independently evidenced, not inferred from booking references.

L21 — External assets and prototype text need presentation QA

PaymentQR uses externally hosted logo/QR assets; unavailable network resources can leave broken images. Some source strings contain a literal question mark where a currency sign appears intended. Audio alert and shift displays do not establish working sound notifications or attendance monitoring.

Next decision: Check offline/network-failure presentation, currency labels, icon accessibility, responsive navigation, and label accuracy. Do not equate a toggle's appearance with a functioning backend capability.

L22 — No accepted automated regression evidence accompanies this snapshot

No dedicated test project or application test suite was identified in the inspected source set. Existing binaries, prior chat statements about builds, and file timestamps do not prove that every user scenario passed. This package supplies a manual acceptance plan rather than manufacturing a pass report.

Next decision: Record executed test dates/results and sanitized evidence. Later add unit/integration tests for price arithmetic, draft persistence, authorization, status transitions, payment locking, and report filters. Separate source implementation dates from actual test dates in the Gantt chart.

L23 — Per-item quantities are not explicit on review and receipt

ReviewBooking.cshtml and BookingReceipt.cshtml render quantity-times-price detail only when charging basis is PerKg. All other lines show unit price and line total without the quantity multiplier. A customer selecting two Powder items can see PHP 10.00 unit price and PHP 20.00 line total without an explicit quantity 2.

The underlying line-total calculation still multiplies quantity by unit price; this is a transparency/presentation gap, not evidence that arithmetic always fails. The receipt also retains estimated-weight/estimated-total wording, and its footer selects a payment message from the payment-method value rather than a full payment verification/balance model. It should not be presented as an authoritative paid receipt after later staff payment or actual-weight changes.

Next decision: Display PerItem quantity, unit price, and line total explicitly; distinguish booking acknowledgment from payment receipt, and label current versus estimated weight correctly. Retest T08 and T17.

L24 — Actual-weight precision is not enforced consistently server-side

The modal requests a 0.01 step, while UpdateActualWeight rejects only <= 0 and > 999.99. It does not explicitly reject more than two decimal places or values between 0 and 0.01. Database weight and service-quantity columns store two decimals, whereas the projected-total calculation uses the submitted decimal value before persistence.

Consequently, crafted requests such as 0.001, 0.005, or 1.234 kg can encounter database rounding, a rounded zero violating a positive-quantity constraint, or disagreement between the pre-save projection and persisted totals. These are source-identified risks; actual behavior depends on database precision and constraint enforcement and has not been executed here.

Next decision: Define the supported measurement precision and minimum, validate/normalize once on the server before both calculations and writes, and give a friendly failure message. Test stored amount, paid-balance protection, audit notes, and notification text together (T27).

L25 — Zero-total GCash confirmation lacks a friendly business rule

Owner pricing permits zero for non-Self-Service services; customer draft validation specifically rejects nonpositive Self Service pricing but does not reject a zero grand total. GCash booking persistence attempts to insert a Pending payment at that draft total. The schema requires payments.amount > 0.

On a database enforcing that constraint, a zero-total submission should fail and roll back the booking transaction. The surrounding generic catch rolls back and rethrows, so the source does not provide a friendly free-order/invalid-price decision. This is not a claim that a zero-price attempt has been run or that partial data was left behind.

Next decision: Either disallow zero-priced bookable services with an explanatory validation response or define a legitimate free-order path that does not insert a zero-value payment. Run T45 against an isolated fixture and verify all related row counts after failure.

9.5 Reusable booking-and-payment explanation

The customer booking process uses a review stage before the system creates the final booking record. Service selections, estimated weight, add-on quantities, and payment preference are held in a session draft and presented for checking. The customer can return to edit the draft or cancel before submission. Cash on Pickup bookings can be confirmed without an immediate payment record. For GCash, the current prototype uses a customer acknowledgement followed by staff verification; it does not connect to a live payment gateway. A booking's service total and its received-payment total are stored or calculated separately so that operational status does not automatically become proof of payment.

9.6 Reusable weight-and-pricing explanation

Customer-entered weight is an estimate used to calculate a preliminary service amount. Staff can record the measured weight at the shop. The system then adjusts quantities for per-kilogram service lines using the unit prices already saved for that booking, while per-piece and fixed-fee lines remain unchanged. A history note records the weight/amount change, and the customer receives an in-app notification. The current schema uses one item-weight field rather than separate estimated and actual weight columns; this is a limitation relevant to auditability and later enhancement.

When explaining a price example, label it as an example rather than a permanent shop tariff. If Washing is PHP 25/kg and Drying is PHP 25/kg, five kilograms of those two services cost PHP 250 before optional charges. At six kilograms they cost PHP 300. A fixed PHP 75 Rush Fee adds PHP 75 once, not PHP 75 for every kilogram. Always verify the current catalog before demonstrating.

9.7 Reusable reporting explanation

The owner has a monthly collection summary and a separate payment-transaction report. The monthly summary groups recorded Paid payments by payment month and method. The transaction report lists individual payment records and provides period, payment-method, and payment-status filters. This lets the owner request a narrower set, such as today's recorded payments, or keep All periods selected. The print action is designed to use the currently rendered report selection. These outputs summarize collections; they do not calculate net profit because operating expenses and stock consumption costs are not currently integrated. Identified filter-visibility and print-layout issues must be validated and corrected before claiming full acceptance.
